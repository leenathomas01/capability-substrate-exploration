# Limitations and known failure modes

This is an **untested proposal**, not an established method.

## Concepts and measurement

- A hidden capability in an implemented model is not evidence for the realizability of an idea without a substrate.
- "Reachable capability" depends on resources, elicitation, and evaluation; negative observations do not prove impossibility.
- Non-additivity depends on outcome scale and reference baseline. Mere performance gains or composing familiar functions need not imply a new capability.
- Shared training data, extra optimization, evaluation leakage, or experimenter-provided integration may simulate synergy.
- Two advances are not privileged mathematically; some interactions require three or more components.

## Methods and economics

- Quality-diversity, novelty search, recombination, and program archives already preserve alternatives; the methodology must outperform relevant controls to demonstrate value.
- Maintaining speculative entries incurs storage, search, verification, and opportunity costs; a write-only archive has no advantage.
- Dependency annotations may be wrong, incomplete, overly broad, or biased toward currently fashionable paradigms.
- Delaying candidate-level cost filters never eliminates finite resource budgets or safety constraints.
- An archive retaining every conjecture may bury valuable entries beneath noise. Retrieval quality is a crucial independent variable.
- Filtering baselines must not be made artificially weak; matched compute and evaluation privileges matter.
- Changing task distributions or standards across stages can manufacture apparent benefits.
- Later primitives could be supplied by the experiment designer, rather than discovered by the system; disclose which question this tests.

## Epistemic and external validity

- Toy-world results may not translate to frontier model R&D, scientific research, or real-world technologies.
- Failure in finite experiments weakens claims within that domain; it does not universally disprove possible interaction effects.
- Success on a small benchmark does not establish open-ended discovery or recursive self-improvement.
- A capability gain is not evidence of desirable deployment economics, safety, or social value.
- Literature mapping is not exhaustive; conceptual overlap does not establish novelty.
